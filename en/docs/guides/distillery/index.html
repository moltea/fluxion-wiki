Vessels, Siphons and Distilling

The distillate workshop: vessels and brands, vials, siphons and their fittings, the Gyre, retorts with alembics, the Holding Chamber, Separator, Mixing Vat, Brewing Tower and Abacus, bands, the Atomizer.

The Crucible brews from one cauldron and forgets everything that was in it. The distillate workshop stores currents for later and carries them by itself: vessels hold, siphons convey, retorts break things into currents with no hands, and the Brewing Tower brews Crucible recipes for as long as it is fed. The Confluence, the Atomizer, lanterns and other devices draw from these stores.

Vessel

A Vessel — glass blown on opaline in rings of old bronze — holds one current, up to 192 parts. A siphon over its neck fills it; a siphon at its side or under its bottom takes from it. Broken by hand, a vessel carries what it holds with it. Entry — Distillate.

  • Brand. A flint and steel on a vessel burns the rune of the current inside into its bronze tag. A branded vessel keeps its current even when empty: it takes nothing else and calls its own along the siphons. An empty vessel can be branded with an orb — the orb goes into the tag. An axe scrapes the brand off.
  • Seal. Bindwax seals the neck: a sealed vessel neither gives nor takes. An axe chips the seal off.
  • Puddle. Tip a vessel over by sneaking with an empty hand — or blow it up — and the distillate lies as a puddle. In ten seconds it dries, and a quarter of what spilled settles into the land as murk.
  • A vessel in hand pours like a big vial: click it on another vessel, an alembic or a Gyre — it takes that current, as much as fits, and if there is nothing to take, it gives its own. To place a vessel next to a holder — sneak.

A Spillway Vessel keeps its own current and passes what it does not take — its own excess and any other current — to the vessel below it. Stack spillway vessels on top of each other, and they sort the currents themselves: each gets its own.

Vial

A Vial carries 8 parts where a siphon cannot reach. A vial on a vessel, an alembic or a Gyre scoops (from a Gyre — its fullest current, into a filled vial — only its own current); when there is nothing to scoop, it pours out — into a vessel, a Gyre or a Crucible. You cannot scoop from a Crucible: it is not a store. A vial thrown into a Crucible dissolves together with what it holds. Entry — Vials.

Siphons

A Siphon is a glass tube in bronze clamps. Distillate flows along it by itself, like water: down and level, never up. A siphon at its side takes from a Crucible, a siphon over its neck fills a vessel — so the vessel stands lower than the Crucible. Entry — Siphons.

  • A siphon takes a part only if further down the tube there is a holder that will accept it right now — and gives it straight there. A brand calls its own current; from a Crucible the fullest of what is awaited leaves first. The nearest holder goes first, equal ones take turns.
  • There are no clogs: a siphon takes nothing of a current with nowhere to go — its drop trembles at the outlet, and the Loupe on the siphon says "no receiver". Set a vessel that accepts it, and it flows.
  • Poured into a Crucible from above, distillate mixes by the rules of the Wheel (clashes and the limit — as with thrown things).

The Gyre

The Gyre is a big shared store: a sphere of blown glass on a bronze foot, two blocks tall, holding up to 1536 parts of any currents. Currents in it neither mix nor clash — each circles as its own strand. A siphon over its neck fills it with any current, a siphon at its side or under its foot takes the fullest of what is awaited below. Set it by a Confluence Heart — the Heart draws from it as from vessels. Broken by hand, the Gyre carries what it holds; an explosion shatters it. Entry — The Gyre.

Distilling: retorts and alembics

Distilling breaks things into currents by itself — no water, no catalyst, no hands. A Retort is a stone hearth under a copper gourd: put a thing with currents in it (by hand or a hopper from above) and stoke the hearth (by hand or a hopper from the side) — the thing breaks down, and its currents rise as vapor into the neck. Entry — Distillation.

  • The currents are caught by Alembic — beaked vessels, up to four in a column on the neck. Each current goes where it already is, otherwise to the first empty one. With nowhere to go, it waits in the retort, and no new thing is taken. Siphons at the sides take from the alembics.
  • Losses: a plain retort loses a sixth of the currents as murk. A Brazier Briquette burns in the hearth four times longer than coal.
  • A Side Outlet right against a retort's side gives one more column of alembics; a Settling Vat nearby catches what is lost as silt instead of murk — eight parts per silt (the entry Side Outlets and the Settling Vat).
  • A Gale Fan with its nose at a retort's side or back fans the hearth: up to three bellows, each two thirds faster on the place's Gale, a third without it.
  • The retort and its column are on the diagram in the Codex; "Place ghost" sets it all in the world.

Holding Chamber, Separator, Mixing Vat

  • The Holding Chamber measures out 12 parts and gives them all at once when the holder below is empty: with one siphon you feed a Crucible in portions — it brews, empties and gets the next. While it gives, it takes nothing from above. Entry — The Holding Chamber.
  • The Separator splits a compound current into its parents: two parts of the compound — a part of each parent (spare Drift becomes Gale and Tide again); a parent that is itself compound splits when it gathers a pair. Siphons at the sides and below take only Springs and Poles. Nothing is lost or born. Entry — The Separator.
  • The Mixing Vat combines without a Crucible: two parts of different currents that combine on the Wheel — a part of the compound. Opposite Springs merge only if the vat stands lower than both holders they flow from — like a basin of the Riverbed puzzle; higher, they clash into murk. Entry — The Mixing Vat.

Brewing Tower and Abacus

The Brewing Tower is a self-brewing Crucible: it brews Crucible recipes by itself as long as it is fed by siphon and given catalysts. It needs neither water nor fire — the chunk's flux heats the brew, and in drained land the tower stands. The milestone entry — The Brewing Tower.

  • How to get it: set a Crucible on a Fluxsteel Retort and throw a pinch of quickdust on it — the retort becomes the firebox, the Crucible the dome. The Crucible's distillate passes into the dome.
  • Memory. Strike the tower with a recipe's catalyst — it remembers the most demanding one you know; sneaking with an empty hand — it forgets the last one. The tower itself remembers one recipe, every Abacus right against its side — two more. Potions are taught with the potion itself. The tower brews no creatures: a glint needs a Crucible.
  • Feeding. A siphon over the dome pours only what the remembered recipes need, no more than for two brews. Catalysts go in by hand or a hopper at the side and top, the brewed things come out by an empty hand or a hopper below.

Bands

A band makes a vessel bigger without pouring it out: put on a vessel, a spillway vessel or an alembic, it keeps what is inside and holds more. A second band does not fit over the first, but a stronger one replaces a weaker one. Entry — Bands.

Atomizer and more

  • The Atomizer gives a potion as a cloud to everyone nearby and pays with distillate, not a bottle: put a sample potion in its flask, a siphon from above pours the potion's currents, a redstone signal — a puff (half the potion's currents). The cloud lives ten seconds; Current Grit in the nozzle keeps it twice as long. In detail — Potion Brewing.
  • The Druse Vat grows shards from distillate after a sample — a Shard Block on its bottom: eight parts of the sample's current and a little flux make a shard (The Druse Vat).
  • The Sluice Box doubles ore: water at its upper edge carries off the waste rock, and the concentrate gives two ingots in a furnace (Ore Panning).
  • Currents in an Applied Energistics 2 ME network — the compatibility branch.
