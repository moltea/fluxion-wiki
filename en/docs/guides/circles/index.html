Current Circles

An ink circle on a field of Flowstone Slabs: how to draw a small and a great circle, what goes into the sockets, field deeds and rites, the Through-Circle.

A current circle is an ink drawing on a field of slabs underfoot. Put currents into its three sockets and the circle does the work itself: tills, reaps, heals, keeps monsters out, smelts, carries you to its pair. It pays with the flux of its own chunk and leaves almost no murk. Codex entries — Current Circles, Current Glyphs, Through-Circles and four rite entries.

How to draw

  1. Lay bottom Flowstone Slab in a square at one height: 3 × 3 — a small circle, 5 × 5 — a great one. The slabs are dry and not taken by another circle.
  2. Run the Ruling Pen over the middle slab. A small circle costs 24 of 96 ink marks, a great one — 48. The circle's arrow points where you face.
  3. A great circle grows from a small one: add sixteen slabs around it and run the pen again — the socketed items stay.

Short of a slab or ink — a mod line says how many. The field plan with a ghost is on the Current Circles page.

  • Hands. Any slab of the circle acts as its middle: an item goes into the first free socket (one item per socket). Sneaking with an empty hand — the last socket back. Sneaking with the Ruling Pen — erase the circle (the slabs stay, the items go to you).
  • Any slab broken — the whole circle falls apart, socketed items drop at the middle. Pistons don't move circle slabs.
  • A redstone signal on any slab puts the circle to sleep — that is how you switch a rite off with a lever.
  • The Loupe over any slab reads the whole circle: socket currents, what it does, why it sleeps.

What goes into the sockets

  • A primal current shard or a distillate vial — good for field deeds.
  • A current glyph — the sign of any of the 42 currents; it never runs out and never breaks. The Crucible casts it from a Glyph Blank (stonecutter: Flowstone → 4): the blank is the catalyst, the distillate holds 4 shares of its current. Won't brew without the Current Glyphs entry.
  • Only glyphs wake rites. A circle whose filled sockets are all glyphs works one block wider.

Socket currents add up along the Wheel: if two currents make a third, the circle reads the sum too. The combination is exact — an extra current throws the circle off ("The sockets add up to nothing").

Strength and radius

The radius comes from the flux of the circle's chunk: under half the riverbed — 2 blocks, up to the riverbed — 3, up to one and a half riverbeds — 4, in flood — 5. An all-glyph circle is one block wider, a great one two more (up to 8). Every deed takes the flux of its chunk, but never below the riverbed floor: on drained land the circle waits.

The Through-Circle

Two circles — an entrance and an exit: stand on one, crouch — and you are on the other.

  1. Sockets — Shift, Drift and Bind glyphs: a lone Through-Circle.
  2. Press one more Bind glyph against it — it remembers the circle (tooltip "Remembers the Through-Circle at x, y, z").
  3. Put it into a second circle's socket together with Shift and Drift — a pair. The far circle may be asleep: the pair is a world record.
  • Step: stand inside the rim and crouch; after a second and a half (30 ticks) you are in the middle of the far circle, facing along its arrow. Step off — canceled. Cooldown — 5 s. Your own pets standing on the circle come along — up to two. The circle carries no items and no other creatures — items go through Through-Mirrors.
  • Cost — flux of the departure chunk: 4 + 1 per 100 blocks, up to 16; between worlds — 24. No murk, no Dissonance.
  • Worlds: a small circle leads within its own world; between the Overworld, the Nether and the End — only great circles on both sides. The circle does not lead into the mod's worlds.
  • Owner — whoever socketed the last glyph. Sneaking, press with any glyph to open the circle to guests or close it.
  • The far land is not kept loaded: it arrives for the moment of the step and leaves on its own.
