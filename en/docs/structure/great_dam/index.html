Great Dam

worldThe Leesdwellers3chests22
The weirkeepers dammed a basin of the Lees to hold the murk itself. The eldest of them still sits on the crest under his gate; behind him the ooze stands to the brim, and below lies the dry bed they drained.

Notes found there

The pages the structure keeps in its rooms; the hints to its locks are among them.

Spoiler: this reveals part of the walkthrough.
  • Rule of the weir: the murk is held, not fought. Walk the lanes only when the winch is still, and stand behind a pile when it turns.
  • Winch book: the head-race over the crest is full to the brim. Every gate below takes its share through the dam, and the dam holds the rest.
  • Keeper of the weir: the eldest stopped sleeping. He sits on the crest under his gate and says the murk will settle if someone keeps it long enough.
  • Last watch: the silt came in through the windows. The others went down into the bed. I hear them singing on the crest at night.
  • Measure basin: the left wheel lets in three spans, the right lets out two. The gate rises when the water stands at the lit mark.
  • Cistern order: the left wheel lets out three spans, the right lets in two. Leave only the lit span, and the gate will rise.
  • Gauge keeper: the left wheel lets in three spans, the right lets out two. We never fill to the brim and never drain to the stone: the gate opens at the lit mark.
  • Under the crest: the strongroom lies beneath the eldest. Its seals answer to him alone; while he keeps the weir, it stays shut.
  • What the weir held: the hub of the old wheel, the measures of every span, and the figure of the first of us.
  • Gauge records: the murk stood at the third mark the year we closed the weir, at the fourth the next. We stopped writing when it passed the last.
  • Offering at the weir: a shell for every span we hold. The eldest says the murk listens; the rest of us just keep the candles lit.
  • Wheelwright: a wheel turned the wrong way floods the chamber. Keep the spares greased, and never mount a wheel you have not counted.
  • Strongbox rule: the signs over the box are read from the left. Step on them in that order, and the seals will know you for one of us.
  • Winch well: the chains run up to the head-race and down to nothing we ever found. Whatever falls here stays here.
  • Night of the silt: it came through the wall while we slept, quiet as breath. By morning half the bunks were under it.
  • Outlet mark: this pipe drained the bowl in four days. Close the wheel before you walk it; the murk remembers the way out.
  • Lampkeeper: a lamp on every pier and one in every alcove where we breathe. Down in the bowl the air is thin; the light shows where it holds.
  • Ray nets: the rays came up the bowl at night for the last of the murk. We caught them by lamplight and dried their barbs here.
  • Sample keeper: one jar from the head-race every tenth day, sealed and marked. In the old jars the murk has settled; in the new ones it stands to the brim.
  • Watch rule: when the murk passes the third mark, ring until the crest answers. Nobody answered the last time.
  • Rod keeper: every staff is cut to the span of the first one. If a new rod does not match the old, the new one is wrong.
  • Watch stores: kelp for the night watch, candles for the long one. Whoever takes the last of anything brings back two.
  • The drowned rest: we lay our dead in the wall of the weir, so that they keep holding it. Some of them still do.
  • Watch roll: one keeper at the bed door from bell to bell, and a light on the board for every watch kept. The last ones were never lit.
  • Gate smith: a gate leaf is cast, never forged. Here we only mend the chains, and the chains always break first.
  • Silt crew: what the weir holds back, the bed lets in from below. Dig it out every day, or the dry bed is not dry.
